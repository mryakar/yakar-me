
I’m Ahmet. I studied mechatronics engineering, but somewhere between the motors and the control loops I realised the part
I liked most was the code. Nine years later I’m a senior backend engineer, building large-scale systems in the defence
industry — mostly Java, mostly distributed and event-driven.

The part of the job I can’t get enough of is concurrency. It’s where software stops being deterministic, and the question
becomes: how do you use a system you can barely control — and use it perfectly well? It is never boring. I write about it
[here](/writing/), along with PostgreSQL internals. I was writing long before either, and not only about software; writing
is simply one of the things that makes me happy.

At work, people tend to come to me when they want something explained — not just what it does, but why it was built that
way, in words that make it click. The conversations rarely stay technical for long. Philosophy, science, books and music
usually find their way in.

I learn on purpose. Most of what I study runs on a plan, and I’ve made peace with being that person. Outside work I play
the saxophone and take photographs. I can lose an entire evening to a video deriving some physics equation, and I think
black holes are the most interesting objects in the universe. I’m happiest in nature and a little less so in crowds. I
love learning languages and travelling — so far a good part of Asia and most of Europe. Football, I have to admit,
remains a complete mystery to me.

Lately I’m most interested in payments and financial systems, where correctness is measured in cents. If you’d like to
talk about backend systems — or anything above — [my inbox is open](mailto:ahmet@yakar.me).
